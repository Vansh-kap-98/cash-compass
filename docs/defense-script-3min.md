## 1 · GLEB — Title — 0:00–0:10

Good morning. We're Penny Pilots — nine students across Russia and India. This
is Cash Compass: a finance app that answers one question students actually ask.

> *26 words*

## 2 · KINTAN — Team, India — 0:10–0:20

Our team in India built the mobile product and the interface — frontend,
design, economics and user research, five of us at Manipal Jaipur.

> *26 words*

---

## 3 · GLEB — Team, Russia — 0:20–0:30

And four of us at Southern Federal University built the backend, the data

> *25 words*

## 4 · ATHENA — The problem — 0:30–0:48

Every finance app tells you where your money went. That's a report, not a
decision.

A student standing in a shop with four days left doesn't need a report. They
need to know: can I afford this, right now? Nothing on the market answers that.
So we built it.

> *47 words*

---

## 5 · GLEB — Key features — 0:48–1:06

Six features, all built around that one question.

Safe-to-Spend gives a daily limit after bills and goals. Reason tags expose
impulse and social spending. Recurring charges get caught automatically. Goals,
calendar alerts and a personalised dashboard complete it.

Three of us built the platforms underneath.

> *46 words*

---

## 6 · KINTAN — Front end — 1:06–1:20

The web app is React and TypeScript, with Tailwind and Recharts, deployed on
Vercel.

It ships in three languages through i18next — because half this team doesn't
speak the other half's language, and neither do our users.

> *36 words*

---

## 7 · GLEB — Back end — 1:20–1:34

Behind both platforms: Python and FastAPI on TiDB, deployed on Railway.
Eighteen endpoints, four tables, responses under a hundred milliseconds.

One backend serves the website and the phone — so the logic exists once, not
twice.

> *36 words*

---

## 8 · VANSH — Mobile — 1:34–1:48

The Android app is Flutter — twenty-three thousand lines of Dart, five hundred
and thirty-seven tests passing.

It works with no account and no connection. Receipts are read on-device, so the
photograph never leaves your phone.

> *36 words*

---

## 9 · ISMAIL — Market research — 1:48–2:08

We compared Cash Compass against the tools students already have.

Forty-three million students in our market. Typical allowance, five to ten
thousand rupees. Only eighteen per cent track their spending at all — and around
sixty-five per cent run out seven to ten days early.

Mint and Wallet are built for salaried users. That gap is the product.

> *52 words*

---

## 10 · AKSHIT then VLAD — Economics & data — 2:08–2:28

**AKSHIT —** Underneath it are five mathematical models. The survival calculator
takes income, fixed costs and upcoming bills, and returns one number: what's
safe to spend today. Green, Tight, or Critical.

**VLAD —** And every figure is derived live from the transaction data — never
cached, never stale. The number you see is the number that's true now.

> *52 words*

---

## 11 · MEEHIKA then SIMPHIWE — UI/UX & design — 2:28–2:46

**MEEHIKA —** Twenty-two high-fidelity screens define the product. Three steps
throughout: capture, understand, decide. The mobile app stays deliberately
restrained so the numbers carry the page.

**SIMPHIWE —** And the brand carries across both platforms and this deck —
one identity, from the app icon to the slide you're looking at.

> *47 words*

Two countries, one product, built in parallel and shipping today. Everything is

## Cue sheet

- **Gleb speaks five times** (1, 3, 5, 7, 12). Practise those re-entries — they
  are where a 3-minute run usually loses ten seconds.
- **Slides 10 and 11 are two-voice.** Hand over mid-slide without pausing; the
  second speaker starts as the first finishes their last word.
- **Nobody says their own name or role.** The slide shows it. Nine introductions
  would cost twenty-five seconds — fourteen per cent of your entire time.
- **Drop first if you're over:** Gleb's last line in §5, Vlad's second sentence
  in §10, Simphiwe's clause after the dash in §11.
- **Say these slowly — they are the credibility beats:** 537 tests · 43.3 million
  · 18 per cent · 65 per cent · 100 milliseconds.
- **At 3 minutes there is no recovery time.** If a slide runs long, the next
  speaker cuts rather than catching up.
